INTRODUCTION

Bid rigging refers to the situation when:

Competitors agree in advance who will win.

01

Complementary bidding

Competitors submit bids designed to lose. A bid may be intentionally high or deliberately nonresponsive.

02

Bid rotation

The same competitors keep bidding, but they agree to take turns submitting the lowest bid.

03

Bid suppression

One or more competitors agree not to bid, or agree to withdraw, so that a selected bidder wins.

04

Customer or market allocation

Competitors divide customers, territories, or types of work and avoid genuine competition outside their assigned area.

Customer or market allocation

04

SUSPICIOUS INDICATORS

The seventeen red flags

1

Winner subcontracts to losers

A winning bidder subcontracts part of the business to one or more losing bidders.

2

Clear gap

A clear gap between the winner and all others — the “number-to-bid-above” situation.

3

Split the contract

Companies appear to have split the contract — each low on some aspect, high on others.

4

Significant increases

Significant increases by most bidders with no substantial cost increase.

  1. 5Identical bids. Identical bids from different companies — line items or lump sum.
  2. 6Bids above estimate. Bids well above the agency’s estimate, or above comparable bids by the same companies elsewhere.
  3. 7Physical alteration. Physical alteration of bids, especially at the last minute.
  4. 8Line items higher. Particular line items much higher for some bidders — no relation to cost.
  5. 9Bids very close. All bids very close together — bidders knew each other’s prices.
  6. 10Same increment. The same increment between each company’s bid.
  7. 11High bids when scarce. All companies bid high when work is known to be scarce.
  8. 12Different bids nearby. A company gives different bids for the same line item on nearby contracts.
  9. 13Physical evidence. Physical evidence of collusion — same handwriting, same envelopes, same math or spelling errors, same fax number.
  10. 14Qualified bidders no-bid. Qualified bidders do not bid, especially if they initially took steps to bid.
  11. 15Re-bid same order. On a re-bid, bidders return in the same order, or some fail to re-bid.
  12. 16Prices drop with new bidder. Prices drop mysteriously when a new bidder appears.
  13. 17Competitors meet. Competitors meet shortly before or after bids are submitted.

The seventeen red flags are the “Suspicious Indicators” from the U.S. DOJ Antitrust Division’s Antitrust Primer for Agents and Procurement Officials — also see the Procurement Collusion Strike Force. A red flag is a reason to look but on its own not proof of collusion.

ACTIVITY CASE 1

Snow plowing

One-season citywide snow and ice services. Bids opened October 7.

BidderSeason price10-wheel plow truck
Northline Snow LLC$214,000$168 per hour
Harbor Plow & Sand$281,500$232 per hour
Pioneer Winter Services$286,900$236 per hour

File facts

  1. 1Riverton advertised the contract for three weeks, and three firms submitted bids.
  2. 2All three bids arrived on time with the required bid deposit and insurance certificate.
  3. 3Riverton requires a 90-minute response time and GPS tracking on every truck.
  4. 4Northline’s garage is eight miles from the city’s public works yard.
  5. 5Northline’s season price is 24 percent below Harbor’s, while Harbor and Pioneer are within 2 percent of each other.
  6. 6Northline cleared the city’s sidewalks last winter, and there are no complaints on file.
  7. 7Two weeks after the award, Northline proposes to subcontract the east-side routes to Harbor Plow & Sand.
  8. 8Last winter Riverton had eleven plowable storms, close to its ten-year average.

Which facts merit a further look?

Marybeth Fleming

Robin Frkal

Carrie Kimball

Answer: facts 5 and 7

Fact 5Northline’s season price is 24 percent below Harbor’s, while Harbor and Pioneer are within 2 percent of each other.

This looks like a clear gap between the winner and all others — the “number-to-bid-above” situation (listed above as Red flag 2).

In this file. Northline’s season price of $214,000 is 24 percent below Harbor’s $281,500, while Harbor and Pioneer sit within 2 percent of each other at $281,500 and $286,900. The hourly truck rates show the same shape: $168 against $232 and $236. The two losing bids cluster together, well clear of the winner, as if both firms knew the number they needed to stay above.

Fact 7Two weeks after the award, Northline proposes to subcontract the east-side routes to Harbor Plow & Sand.

This looks like a winning bidder subcontracting part of the business to one or more losing bidders (listed above as Red flag 1).

In this file. Two weeks after winning, Northline proposes to hand the east-side routes to Harbor Plow & Sand, a firm that bid and lost. A subcontract to a losing bidder can be the losing bidder’s reward for staying high. Read it together with Fact 5: the firm that stayed well above the winner now receives part of the work.

ACTIVITY CASE 2

Elevator maintenance and repairs

Two-year full-service agreement for municipal buildings.

Bidder2-year maintenanceRepair laborEmergency call
Atlas Elevator$118,400$235 per hour$1,450 per call
Meridian Lift$161,900$148 per hour$1,520 per call
Summit Vertical$158,300$229 per hour$780 per call

File facts

  1. 1The agreement covers 14 elevators and 2 wheelchair lifts in six municipal buildings.
  2. 2Riverton awards each of the three line items separately to the lowest eligible bidder.
  3. 3All three firms hold state elevator licenses and attended the optional site visit.
  4. 4Two of the buildings have elevators more than 30 years old that need frequent repairs.
  5. 5Meridian’s repair crew is based in Riverton, while Atlas and Summit dispatch from about 25 miles away.
  6. 6Each firm is lowest on exactly one line item and well above the others on the other two.
  7. 7The maintenance scope and response times match the city’s previous agreement.

Which facts merit a further look?

Marybeth Fleming

Robin Frkal

Carrie Kimball

Answer: fact 6

Fact 6Each firm is lowest on exactly one line item and well above the others on the other two.

This looks like companies splitting the contract — each low on some aspect, high on others (listed above as Red flag 3).

In this file. Because Riverton awards each line item separately (Fact 2), the pattern hands every firm one piece of the work. Atlas wins maintenance at $118,400 against $158,300 and more. Meridian wins repair labor at $148 an hour against $229 and more. Summit wins emergency calls at $780 against $1,450 and more. On each line, the two firms that are not meant to win are far above the one that is.

ACTIVITY CASE 3

Outdoor basketball court construction

Two courts, drainage, paving, fencing, lighting, and acrylic surfacing.

BidderOak Street, 2024Riverside Park, 2026Change
Pace Siteworks$316,000$412,000+30.4%
Greenline Civil$329,500$431,000+30.8%
Metro Court Systems$341,000$447,000+31.1%

File facts

  1. 1The Riverside Park courts have the same size, layout, and specifications as the 2024 Oak Street courts.
  2. 2All three firms also bid on the Oak Street project in 2024.
  3. 3The city’s cost index for court materials rose 5 percent between the two bids; every bid rose about 30 percent.
  4. 4Pace is based in Riverton and has built four municipal courts in the region since 2020.
  5. 5The acrylic surface requires a specialty crew, and each bid names its surfacing subcontractor.
  6. 6Greenline’s and Metro’s bid forms contain the same misspelling, “acyrlic,” on the same line.
  7. 7All three bids arrived between 1:10 and 1:45 p.m., before the 2:00 p.m. deadline.
  8. 8The parks department wants the courts finished before the youth league starts in June.

Which facts merit a further look?

Marybeth Fleming

Robin Frkal

Carrie Kimball

Answer: facts 3 and 6

Fact 3The city’s cost index for court materials rose 5 percent between the two bids; every bid rose about 30 percent.

This looks like significant increases by most bidders with no substantial cost increase (listed above as Red flag 4).

In this file. The courts are the same (Fact 1) and so are the bidders (Fact 2). The city’s cost index for court materials rose only 5 percent, yet every firm raised its price by 30 to 31 percent. When all the bids move up together by far more than costs, the firms may have agreed to raise prices.

Fact 6Greenline’s and Metro’s bid forms contain the same misspelling, “acyrlic,” on the same line.

This looks like physical evidence of collusion — same handwriting, same envelopes, same math or spelling errors, same fax number (listed above as Red flag 13).

In this file. Two supposedly independent bid forms contain the same unusual misspelling, “acyrlic,” on the same line. A shared error suggests that one firm prepared or copied the other’s paperwork, or that both worked from a common draft.
